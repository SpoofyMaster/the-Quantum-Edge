## What v8 did
A reversal needed one `REJECT` after T1: one candle that leaves the zone, or (v8) one candle that closes back above
it. So T1, one green reaction candle, then two closes below the T1 wick was a reversal. Two v8 test sequences are
exactly that (`late_t1`, `one_candle_reaction`).

## v9 (MQL5 only; the research defaults and the parity set unchanged)
**Detector.** `revRejCandles` (EA `InpRevRejCandles`, EA default 2; detector default 0 = v8).
- **The count.** After the T1 candle, a candle in the BPR's direction (green for a bullish BPR, red for a bearish
  one) that does not close beyond the reversal level is a rejection candle (`nRejC`). The candles need not follow each
  other.
- **What does not count.** The T1 candle itself, a doji, and a candle that closes beyond the level (price is already
  through the BPR).
- **Price coming back too early.** A close beyond the level that is not the breakout restarts the count: "they must
  be ... before price comes back for breakout". After the review (below): that close is no touch, and a failed
  breakout keeps the count.
- **The breakout.** Step 5b also needs `nRejC ≥ revRejCandles`. The `REJECT` is still needed
  (`InpRevNeedRejection`). Continuation setups are not affected.
- **Records.** Each record carries the count (`rejC`).

**Executor (9.00).**
- The input `InpRevRejCandles` (0..100).
- Log text: a reversal `REJECT` shows `rejection candles: n, counted after T1 and after the last close beyond the
  reversal level; the breakout needs N`; a reversal `BREAKOUT` shows `n rejection candles before it`; the warm-up line
  shows the count.
- Chart: `C1`, `C2`, … on the counted candles (`TrackRejCandles`), as in the owner's drawing.
- The rules row, version strings, `BFEA9_*` CSV files.

**Harness.** Key `rev_rej_c`. With `ext=1` it prints `rc`.

**Chart.** `tools/bk_preview/draw_rejection.py` writes `research/indicators/preview/bpr_rejection_example.svg`, the
owner's C1 / C2 example with the v9 defaults:

  100.61 (100 % = 101.60, 0 % = 98.20);

## Adversarial review of v9 (workflow: 3 reviewers + skeptics)
**How it ran.** Three reviewers read v9 (commit 05a32d4): fidelity to the owner's rule and screenshot, the detector's
state machine, and the executor. The top two findings of each went to a skeptic, who tried to refute them with the
C++ harness.
- Four skeptics first failed on a usage limit. On the owner's "Try again" the workflow was resumed: the finished
  agents replayed from cache and the four skeptics ran again.
- **Result:** 5 findings confirmed, 1 refuted, 3 not sent to a skeptic. Every confirmed finding is fixed (commit
  below).

**Confirmed and fixed (detector).**
- **A failed breakout restarted the count** (fidelity, major).
  - Case: T1, C1, C2, a breakout (2 rejection candles), then one candle back above the T1 wick (`BREAK_FAIL`). The
    count restarted, so the second close below the wick was no breakout and the owner's own setup never traded. v8
    traded it to its target.
  - The owner's rejection came before price came back, so a fresh rejection is not in his rule.
  - Fix: the count block skips bars that start in `BREAK`, and only a close beyond the level that is **not** the
    breakout restarts the count. The deferred flag `rejCReset` is gone: the restart happens at once, after step 5b.
  - Sequence `failed_break_keeps_count`: BREAKOUT 22, BREAK_FAIL 23, BREAKOUT 24, CONFIRM 25, target 28.
- **A too-early close became T2 and moved the level** (detector, minor).
  - A close beyond the level refused by the count fell through to step 6. After a rejection it was counted as T2, and
    its wick became the reversal level: at once with `LAST`, at its visit's rejection with `DEEPEST` (the EA default).
    A candle crashing through the whole BPR was drawn as "T2", and its low (below the BPR) became the level.
  - Fix: with `revRejCandles > 0`, such a close (`early`) is no touch. A visit it opens is a pullback visit, so the
    level stays.
  - Tests: `reset_after_early_break` and `one_green_then_break` now run with DEEPEST, T1 and LAST, plus a crash
    through the BPR.

**Confirmed and fixed (executor).**
- **`BREAK_FAIL` log line** (detector + executor; the executor reviewer rated it major, its skeptic minor). It printed
  the count after the restart as "n rejection candles before it", which read like a breakout after 1 candle. Now only
  a `BREAKOUT` prints its count.
- **C labels with `InpConfirmCloses ≥ 3`** (minor). The labels of a breakout still in progress were deleted on its
  second close. Since a breakout no longer restarts the count, `TrackRejCandles` now deletes labels only when the count
  drops (a too-early close), and the flag array is gone.
- Checked with a Python port of `TrackRejCandles` on a harness dump of the count per bar:
  - confirm closes 2: 532 breakouts;
  - confirm closes 3: 635 breakouts.

  The labels standing at each breakout are exactly the counted candles.

**Not sent to a skeptic, fixed (reading).**
- **The REJECT log text** said "since T1", but after a too-early close the count starts again. Reworded.
- **The `rejC` comment** said "before this bar's candle"; it is the count after the bar.
- **README:** "What to send back" and the CSV headings still said `BFEA8_*`.

**Refuted (kept).**
- **The claim** (fidelity, major): green candles printed after price came back into the zone should not count. Two
  examples:
  - T1, C1, a red candle back into the BPR, a tiny green candle in the BPR, then the break;
  - a shallow T1, one green candle, a deep tap, one green candle, then the break.

  The proposed fix was to restart the count at every new tap.
- **The skeptic's verdict:** both cases have 2 green candles after T1, not one after the other, before the breakout:
  "a minimum 2 candles successive or not its not matter". The proposed restart at every tap would also block a T2
  pullback after a valid rejection, which is the owner's v7 setup ("t1 + rejection + pullback + breakout"). Kept as
  is.
- **Also kept, not verified (an owner decision if he wants it):** a green hammer later in the T1 visit, deeper than
  T1, counts as a rejection candle. A hammer is also a classic rejection candle.
